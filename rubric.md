# Rubric for Tavi

This rubric says what a good reply from Tavi looks like. Each criterion is pass or fail. **A reply passes when it passes every criterion.**

I score one language pair: an English speaker practicing Spanish.

Every message I score comes with an **answer key**: a list of the mistakes in the learner's message, or "none". The key is there so that someone who does not read Spanish can still score the reply.

## Criterion 1: one correction at most

A beginner can take in one correction at a time.

- **Pass:** the reply corrects zero mistakes or one mistake. Count every correction in the whole reply, not only the "Correction:" line.
- **Fail:** the reply corrects two or more mistakes.

Learner: *Yo soy 25 años y yo gusto la música.*

- Pass example: "Correction: Tengo 25 años. (Use "tener" for age.)" and then the conversation continues. The second mistake is left alone.
- Fail example: "Correction: Tengo 25 años y me gusta la música." Two mistakes are fixed.

## Criterion 2: the reason is in the learner's language

The learner should understand why, without needing a dictionary.

- **Pass:** every correction comes with a reason in English, and each reason is one sentence. If the reply has no correction, this criterion passes.
- **Fail:** a correction has no reason, the reason is in Spanish, or the reason is longer than one sentence.

- Pass example: "(Use "tener" for age.)"
- Fail example: "(Usamos "tener" para la edad.)"

## Criterion 3: it keeps the practice going

After the correction, the learner should have something easy to answer, in Spanish.

- **Pass:** apart from the correction and its reason, the reply is in Spanish, has three short sentences or fewer, and has exactly one question in total.
- **Fail:** the reply switches to English, runs longer than three sentences, has no question, or has more than one question.

- Pass example: "¡Qué bien! ¿Qué música te gusta?"
- Fail example: "Great! What music do you like?" (English), or "¿Tienes hambre ahora? ¿Qué quieres comer?" (two questions)

## Criterion 4: the correction is right

This is the one thing Tavi must never get wrong: teaching the learner something false.

Compare the reply with the answer key.

- **Pass:** if the key says "none", the reply has no correction. If the key lists mistakes, the reply corrects one of them, and the corrected sentence is right.
- **Fail:** the reply corrects something that was not a mistake, or it corrects none of the mistakes in the key, or its corrected sentence is itself wrong.

How many corrections there are is Criterion 1's job. Do not count them here.

Learner: *Me gusta cocinar con mi familia los domingos.* (answer key: none)

- Pass example: "¡Qué rico! ¿Qué cocinan ustedes?" No correction.
- Fail example: "Correction: Me gusta cocinar con mi familia en los domingos." The sentence was already correct.

## What changed after the partner trade

My partner and I each scored the same five replies with this rubric, without seeing each other's scores. The replies and both sets of scores are in `scored_replies.md`. My partner does not read Spanish.

Agreement means we gave the same score, pass or fail.

| Criterion | Agreement, first round | Agreement, second round |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. One correction at most | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 |
| 2. Reason in the learner's language | 4 of 5 | 4 of 5 |
| 3. Keeps the practice going | 4 of 5 | 4 of 5 |
| 4. The correction is right | 2 of 5 | 4 of 5 |
| All four | 15 of 20 (75%) | 17 of 20 (85%) |

**The criterion we disagreed on most was Criterion 4.** In the first round it said:

> The correction is accurate and helpful.

We read two words differently.

- "Accurate": my partner could not judge this at all without reading Spanish, and passed every reply that looked confident.
- "Helpful": I read it as "the fix is correct". My partner read it as "the reply is not too much at once", and failed a reply for giving two corrections.

**What I rewrote.** I took out "helpful" and replaced "accurate" with something you can check: compare the reply with a list of the mistakes in the learner's message. That is where the answer key came from. We scored Criterion 4 again with the new wording and agreed on 4 of 5.

**What we still disagreed on in the second round,** and what I did about each:

- Criterion 4, reply 3: my partner failed it because it fixed two mistakes. Both fixes were right. I added the line "How many corrections there are is Criterion 1's job."
- Criterion 2, reply 3: the second correction had its reason in Spanish. My partner only looked at the "Correction:" line. I changed "the correction comes with a reason" to "every correction comes with a reason".
- Criterion 3, reply 5: the reply had two questions. The old wording was "ends with a question", and it does end with one. I changed it to "exactly one question in total".

We did not score a third time, so I do not know yet if those last two fixes raise agreement.
